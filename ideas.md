# 1980's Springfield RP — Design Direction

## Three Initial Directions

### Theme Name: County Record Office
Very Brief Intro: A restrained civic-publication system inspired by county offices, municipal notices, police brochures, and official stationery. It feels established, practical, and grounded in Springfield's 1980 setting.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Main Street Gazette
Very Brief Intro: An editorial newspaper-inspired interface with strong columns, issue labels, bulletin notices, and local-reporting rhythm. It makes the community feel like a town with an active history.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Patrol Desk Terminal
Very Brief Intro: A darker operations-room direction using dispatch-console cues, amber indicators, and restrained utility styling. It is more procedural and tactical while remaining period-authentic rather than futuristic.
Probability: 0.02

## Chosen Direction: County Record Office

### Design Movement
1980s American civic modernism: the visual language of municipal government handbooks, sheriff's office brochures, county filing systems, printed public notices, and official recruitment material—translated into a clean responsive web interface.

### Core Principles
1. Every section should look like an official record, notice, directory, or public-information page rather than a marketing landing page.
2. Structure should feel human and administrative: strong rules, indexed labels, document headings, department marks, and deliberate alignment.
3. The 1980s setting is communicated through print texture, paper tones, serif headlines, typewriter accents, muted civic colors, and period terminology—not neon or nostalgia effects.
4. Modern usability remains non-negotiable: readable contrast, responsive layouts, clear navigation, usable buttons, and restrained motion.

### Color Philosophy
Use warm archival paper as the base, ink navy for institutional authority, forest green for public-service identity, oxblood for official warnings and emphasis, and a sun-faded brass accent for seals and date stamps. The palette should feel printed and durable, as though it belongs on a county noticeboard or a department handbook that has been handled for years.

### Layout Paradigm
Use an indexed civic-publication structure instead of a centered startup hero. The homepage opens with a split editorial lead: a strong title block and a documentary visual, followed by a two-column public-information board. Inner pages use an office-directory rhythm with section rails, numbered headings, compact metadata, and wide reading columns. On mobile, the index rail becomes a collapsible section marker rather than a stack of oversized cards.

### Signature Elements
1. A Springfield civic seal motif built from a simple mountain-and-star mark, used in the masthead, favicon, and page section stamps.
2. Horizontal rule systems, issue/date stamps, and small uppercase official labels to create an archival document cadence.
3. Department directory entries styled as reusable records with insignia blocks, leadership lines, recruitment status, and application actions.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should behave like handling a well-organized public record: direct, legible, and calm. Hover states use ink shifts, underlines, and slight paper lift rather than glowing effects. Navigation should preserve context, and menus should open quickly with clear focus states. Placeholder actions should be clearly marked and never pretend to be live functionality.

### Animation
Use only subtle transitions under 240ms: navigation underline movement, record-row elevation, disclosure expansion, and status indicator fade. Page sections may reveal with a short opacity-and-translate entrance when motion is allowed, but the site must remain fully understandable with reduced motion enabled. No looping decorative animation, parallax, or theatrical text effects.

### Typography System
Pair **Libre Baskerville** for formal page titles and civic section headings with **IBM Plex Sans** for body copy, navigation, labels, and metadata. Use **IBM Plex Mono** sparingly for date stamps, record numbers, recruitment codes, and utility labels. Titles should be compact and editorial; body copy should use comfortable line-height and avoid all-caps paragraphs.

### Brand Essence
1980's Springfield RP is an established Roblox ERLC roleplay organization for players who want structured, period-authentic public-safety, civilian, and community storytelling in a believable American town.

Personality adjectives: **credible, neighborly, organized**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines sound like public notices, department handbooks, and local civic reporting. CTAs are clear and practical; microcopy is welcoming without hype. Avoid generic gaming language and never describe the experience as FiveM—the experience is Roblox ERLC.

Example lines:
- “A working town, a defined era, and a place for every role.”
- “Review the departments, read the notice, and report for duty.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a compact civic seal: a dark green circular field containing a simplified mountain ridge, a small north star, and three short radiating lines that suggest public service. The wordmark uses a condensed serif treatment with a narrow rule beneath “SPRINGFIELD,” while “ROLEPLAY” appears as a small stamped subline. The mark must remain recognizable without text and work as a favicon.

### Signature Brand Color
**Springfield Service Green — #315A48.** It is the ownable color of the organization: more grounded than police blue, more civic than forest green, and evocative of department vehicles, park signage, and printed public-service materials.

## Content and Platform Decisions

The site is being built for a **Roblox ERLC experience**, not FiveM. Any inherited FiveM wording in the supplied refinement brief is treated as superseded. Server status is prepared with a clearly labeled Roblox ERLC integration seam and uses honest unavailable states rather than invented live numbers. Content is separated into structured data modules for departments, board members, roleplay divisions, announcements, rules, applications, and site settings so a future CMS can replace the data layer without rebuilding page components.

The supplied reference images are treated as the visual ground truth for tone: Roblox ERLC public-safety vehicles and characters, High Rock Park scenery, civic signage, patrol scenes, and the existing community-directory structure. The resulting site will use those references selectively and will not reproduce Discord's dark UI as the primary website layout.
